In March 1922, six people vanished from a small farmstead in Bavaria. Andreas
Gruber, his wife Cäzilia, their widowed daughter Viktoria, and her four
children had not been seen for several days when a neighbor grew concerned
and went to check on them. What that neighbor found at Hinterkaifeck became
one of the most studied unsolved cases in German criminal history.

In the weeks before, the household had reported strange occurrences.
Footprints appeared in fresh snow, leading toward the farmhouse but never
away from it. A newspaper no one recognized turned up in the kitchen. Keys
went missing from locked doors. Days before the family disappeared, Andreas
Gruber told a neighbor he had heard footsteps in the attic, though the house
was searched and found empty.

Investigators later determined the family had been killed and their bodies
concealed in the barn. Despite an extensive investigation and dozens of
suspects considered over the following century, no one was ever charged. The
case remains formally open in Bavarian police records to this day.
